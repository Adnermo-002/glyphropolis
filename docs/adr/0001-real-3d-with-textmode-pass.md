# Real 3D world with a textmode pass, not DDA raycasting

The City must feel like a real place you walk through, and expressiveness is the project's prime directive, so the world is true 3D geometry rendered by Three.js and converted to glyphs in a single GLSL fullscreen pass; the glyph grid, per-glyph fg/bg color, and CRT toggle all live in that pass (glyph = f(material x lighting x surface gradient), the LUT idea borrowed from asciicker). A DDA raycaster one ray per glyph column, as proven by ascii-city-walkable, was the serious alternative: it is lighter, dependency-free, and gives per-cell glyph control, but it boxes the world into axis-aligned cells with bounded height, makes smooth movement and real lighting much harder, and spends its CPU budget exactly where our expressiveness ceiling sits, so it was rejected. Consequences: we accept the Three.js dependency and a low-resolution render target as the cell grid (which also makes dynamic resolution scaling trivial), and glyph legibility becomes a first-class design problem handled inside the pass, not by hand-placed art.
